MIDI bytes. Audio samples.

Experimental 31,250-baud encoder. Sends note messages as a stereo waveform through the selected sound output.

This needs compatible interface hardware.A headphone DAC is not a MIDI output. AC coupling, voltage, filtering and resampling can prevent a synth from reading this. The waveform has been checked digitally; the physical connection has not. Keep speakers and headphones disconnected when using this mode.

Requests a 96 kHz audio context. This does not guarantee a 96 kHz DAC path. Uses stereo channel difference, with zero output between messages.

Uses your system output unless this browser supports device selection.

Standard MIDI works with 12 EDO. Other tunings require MPE; the synth must use a ±48-semitone member-channel bend range.

PLAYING NOTES

Drag a finger to move between notes. Hold latches released notes; tap it again to release them. Stop releases everything. Turn your phone sideways for a wider keyboard.

Computer keyboard: A–K and Q–I play two rows. Z / X shift octaves. Space toggles Hold. Escape stops notes.

About the audio experiment

The encoder serializes complete MIDI messages as 8N1 UART, least significant bit first, at 31,250 bits/s. In nominal Type A mode, the right audio channel is positive and the left negative for current-on bits; Type B reverses them. These are normalized audio samples, not specified MIDI voltages or current. A polarity choice cannot convert a DAC into a MIDI interface. Use an appropriate level-conversion/interface circuit, or use USB MIDI for standard hardware control.

Only the internal synth and an MPE-enabled destination can play the non-12-EDO tunings in this version. Ordinary MIDI output remains available for 12 EDO. No recordings or permissions are sent to a server.
